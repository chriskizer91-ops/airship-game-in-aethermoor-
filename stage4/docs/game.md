# The game: Skies of Aethermoor

The game is one page, `dist/game.html`, with everything inside it, so it works with no internet. It runs on a laptop and on a phone.

## How it goes

1. **The title screen.** Your ship flies slowly over Aethermoor at sunset, behind the game's name (see [The title screen](#the-title-screen)). Choose your skies, the game's difficulty (see [The skies](#the-skies)), then **Set sail** straight away, or go **To port** first.
2. **The port.** Your ship turns slowly in a quiet void, on a round stone berth with a brass rim (Chris: a racing game's garage); drag her to turn her round. Pick which ship to sail, buy ships and upgrades with Crystal Shards, and set where the crystals' power goes (see [The port](#the-port)).
3. **A voyage.** Set sail over Aethermoor and fight off waves of raiders, each harder than the last (see [A voyage](#a-voyage)). Downed raiders spill Crystal Shards; fly through them to gather them. After each wave, sail on for more or go back to port to keep what you've gathered. If your ship goes down, the crew get her home with half.

On Crosswinds and the Maelstrom the raiders fight smarter (see [Tactics](#tactics)).

## The skies

Three difficulties, named for the weather:

| Skies | Raiders | Shards |
|---|---|---|
| **Fair Winds** | Aim much worse, reload slower, hit less than half as hard, about a quarter less sturdy, sail slower; a raider captain is no tougher than her crew, hits no harder and reloads no faster. They lose you in a cloud unless they're within 180 m. A broadside's warning lasts a whole second, and they fight simply. **Never a storm** | as earned |
| **Crosswinds** | As described in [The raiders](#the-raiders); they lose you in a cloud unless they're within 250 m. A broadside's warning lasts 0.8 s; big raiders try to cross your bow and rake you. **Storms on waves 8 and 13** | a quarter more |
| **Maelstrom** | Aim a little better, reload a little faster, hit 15% harder, 15% sturdier, an extra Skiff or Cutter in every wave from the third; they lose you in a cloud unless they're within 350 m. A broadside's warning lasts 0.6 s; big raiders try to cross your bow, small ones come at your stern, and one your loaded broadside is pointed at may climb or dive out of it. **Storms on waves 4, 8 and 12** (the Man-o'-war's wave in a storm), and two waves in five after the fifteenth | 60% more |

The title screen remembers your best wave on each. A brand-new Captain starts on **Fair Winds**, the skies marked best for a first voyage; once you've chosen, the game keeps your choice.

## The title screen

- **Your ship over Aethermoor at sunset.** The ship you sail flies a slow, gentle circle 700 m up over the Hearthsea and the island city, leaning into her turn, with the view drifting along beside her. The sun sits low in the west-south-west with rays fanning out of it, the clouds below are peach and gold, and the land lies in warm shadow. Far off, raiders with rust-red sails cross the sky on their own business, just above the horizon: as one crosses out of sight the next comes in, so there's nearly always one to see (three of them on a laptop or a phone held sideways, two on a phone held upright). Wherever she is in the view, each is turned about 30 degrees from side-on to you, her bow towards you as she crosses to the left or her stern as she crosses to the right, so you see her sails glowing in the low sun (seen straight from the side, square sails are edge-on and you'd see only bare masts). On a phone held upright they're small and sail just over the horizon, so they look properly far off. They never fight here. Drag the sky to swing the view round her; let go, and a moment later it eases back to show the sun again. Her ship always sits clear of the title's card, whatever the size of the screen.
- **The sunset is the title's own.** As soon as you set sail (or go to port), the sky goes back to the voyages' late afternoon, exactly as it always is at sea.
- **The skies tint it.** **Fair Winds**: a clear evening, less cloud, more of the land showing. **Crosswinds**: breezy, a little more cloud, and the clouds racing past (the big clouds and the cloud below on the same wind). **Maelstrom**: storm cloud rolling in, dark slate clouds below, a darker sky over a burning horizon, and the clouds racing harder. The change blends in over a second and a half.
- **Fewer taps.** The big button sets sail at once in your ship. For a new Captain it just says **Set sail** (there's nothing to buy yet), and Fair Winds is marked **Best for your first voyage** and already chosen, so Set sail takes a newcomer out on it; that first voyage opens with "Your first voyage, Captain". Back from the sea, it says **Set sail in the Zephyr** (or whichever ship you sail), and a gold line shows the shards waiting to be spent in port (or that you have none yet). **To port** is the plain button beside it. On a phone held sideways the two buttons sit side by side under the skies, and the game's name fits on one line. On a small phone the opening line is left out: held upright, so your ship still shows above the panel; held sideways with the browser's bars showing, so Set sail still shows.
- **Between screens** (the title, the port and the sea) the picture dips into the night for a moment and comes up out of it over half a second, so you never see the wrong scene flash past.
- **It's smooth on a phone**: the title is drawn in fewer goes than a voyage (on a phone about 34 against a voyage's 47 or so): each raider far off is drawn in one go rather than a dozen, and on a phone only her sails, masts, hull and deck cast shadows here (the shadows of the little parts can't be seen at this size). Each frame costs about what a frame at sea does (the check times both on a phone-sized screen, and the title may take at most a tenth longer). The check times them in turns, a voyage then the title, then the other way round, six times, and goes by the middle of the six turns, so a machine busy with other work slows both alike and can't trip it.

## The port

- **Ships.** You start with the Skiff, the Zephyr. The Cutter (Gale) costs ◆ 300, the Brig (Tradewind) ◆ 900 and the Frigate (Tempest) ◆ 2,200. Then come your late-game ships, goals for many voyages: the Galleon (Doldrums) for ◆ 12,000 and the Man-o'-war (Thunderhead) for ◆ 30,000 (see [The Galleon and the Man-o'-war](#the-galleon-and-the-man-o-war)). The six sit along the bottom: in one row on a laptop, or two rows of three on a narrow window and on a phone either way (there just their names and prices: her class is in the panel). Tap a ship to look at it; if it's yours, it's the one you sail. Looking at a ship you don't own, the big gold button buys her ("Buy the Tempest · ◆ 2,200", always on one line on a phone, with the price in one piece; on the smallest phone held sideways, "Buy Tempest · ◆ 2,200"); until you can afford her it fills with gold as you earn towards her price, and says how many shards you have. **The Man-o'-war is sold only once you own the Galleon**: until then her button says "Buy the Doldrums first" and can't be pressed. (Owning her alone, your treasure ships would still be treasure Brigs, and a laden Brig outruns a Man-o'-war.) Under a giant's button a line says what owning her brings ("Once she's yours, raiders sail Men-o'-war too"; on a laptop and a phone held upright, where there's room), and as you buy her a note at the top says it again, on any screen (on a phone held sideways, on two lines beside the panel, never under it). Setting sail in your own ship is the plain line under it ("or set sail in the Zephyr").
- **How she sails.** Bars for top speed, turning, climbing, hull and firepower, against the best any ship can be, in plain words: top speed in km/h, turning as the time for a full circle, climbing in metres a second, and firepower as light, fair, heavy, very heavy or (more than a tenth past a Frigate at her best) fearsome: the Galleon as built is very heavy, the Man-o'-war fearsome. Your Man-o'-war's hull and broadside are some twenty to fifty times a Skiff's, so those two bars are squeezed: a Skiff's still shows, a bigger ship's is always longer, and the number or the word says exactly how much. Pale is the ship as built; gold past it is what upgrades add, and red is what they cost. Looking at another ship, a white mark on each bar shows where the ship you sail now is, to compare. Her guns are listed in words too ("Guns: 2 in the bow, 6 each side, 1 in the stern"); on a narrow phone the line breaks only between its pieces, never inside one.
- **On a phone**, looking at a ship you own, the panel has two tabs: **The ship** (what she's like and how she sails) and **Upgrades** (the upgrades first, then crystal power). A gold dot on Upgrades means there's one you can afford. Held upright, the panel is only as tall as what's in it, and your ship shows as big as fits in the room above it (all of her above the panel, berth and all); the ship's tab of a ship you own leaves out the heading over how she sails (the tab says it), so all five bars show above Set sail (on a small phone, the first two, with the rest a scroll away). The Upgrades tab makes the panel taller, with the ship smaller above it, and so does looking at a ship you don't own, so all of how she sails shows above the Buy button (on a small phone, without the line about your shards under it). Held sideways, the panel is on the right and the ships along the bottom on the left, in two rows of three (on the smallest phones, an iPhone SE's first one, the panel is a little narrower and the ships' buttons a little tighter, so all six fit); how she sails comes first (without its heading: the tab says it), then what she's like, all above the button, and the Upgrades tab leaves her class and guns to the ship's tab, so two upgrades show above Set sail (on the shortest screens one, with the rest just a scroll away). On a very short screen, with the browser's bars showing, what she's like is left out. On a phone held upright the button at the top says just the skies' name.
- **Try her out.** Under the Buy button of a ship you don't own, a plain button takes her out for a sea trial, like a test drive (see [A sea trial](#a-sea-trial)).
- **Crystal power** (free, change it any time). A slider from Sails to Guns, in five notches. Each notch towards the guns gives 8% faster reloading and 6% heavier shots, and costs 6% top speed and 6% speeding up. Towards the sails, the other way round.
- **Upgrades.** Four of them, each bought in three steps, for each ship separately. Each says what it does in plain words, with the exact numbers in small print under it:

| Upgrade | In port it says | Each step |
|---|---|---|
| **Armour plates** | A tougher hull, but a little slower. | +20% hull, but 4% less top speed and 10% slower to speed up |
| **Fine canvas** | Stronger sails, and a little faster. | +20% sails and 4% more top speed |
| **Gun drill** | Your gunners reload faster. | Guns reload 10% faster |
| **Cut crystals** | Stronger crystals, and she climbs faster. | +20% crystals and climbs 12% faster |

Steps cost ◆ 60, 140 and 280 on the Skiff, and more on bigger ships: 1.6 times as much on the Cutter, 2.6 times on the Brig, 4 times on the Frigate, 7 times on the Galleon (◆ 420, 980 and 1,960) and 10 times on the Man-o'-war (◆ 600, 1,400 and 2,800).

### A sea trial

Like a garage's test drive: **Try her out**, under the Buy button of any ship you don't own yet (the Galleon and the Man-o'-war too, before you've earned a shard), takes her out for one fight that suits her: the Gale against wave 4's Cutter and Skiff, the Tradewind against wave 5's raider captain, the Tempest against wave 8's Frigate and two Cutters, the Doldrums against wave 13's two Frigates and a Brig, and the Thunderhead against wave 15's raider captain's Frigate, Frigate, Brig and Cutter. She sails as she's built (no upgrades, crystal power even), in your skies, in clear weather. Her banner says "Sea trial: the Doldrums", and so does the wave's ("Sea trial: raiders").

- **It earns and costs nothing, and changes nothing in your save**: her wrecks spill no shards and no bounty rises, nothing is banked, and no voyage or best wave is counted; your shards, ships, upgrades and skies stay just as they were.
- **It never counts as owning her**: the raiders are those of the ships you really own, so trying the Galleon or the Man-o'-war never brings giant raiders (wave 15 brings a raider captain's Frigate in the Man-o'-war's place, as it does before you own her).
- **Then home**: win, and a card says "Sea trial over: she won", with only Back to port (it goes by itself after 25 seconds); sink, and the card says it cost nothing, with To port; or pause and go back to port. Each brings you back to the same port screen, looking at her, with a line at the top saying what she costs ("Back from her sea trial: the Doldrums costs ◆ 12,000").

### The Galleon and the Man-o'-war

Your late-game ships (Chris, October 6: until then they were only ever raiders). They cost what many voyages bring home: the whole port before them (ships and every upgrade) came to about ◆ 21,000, and with them it's about ◆ 96,000. By our reckoning, a good Captain on Crosswinds who gathers her shards and brings them home reaches the Galleon around her eighth to tenth voyage (a voyage in a Frigate to wave 10 or 12 brings home ◆ 4,000 to 7,500, and she'll buy some upgrades on the way), and the Man-o'-war around her fifteenth, after a few voyages in the Galleon.

| | Galleon (Doldrums) | Man-o'-war (Thunderhead) |
|---|---|---|
| Price | ◆ 12,000 | ◆ 30,000 |
| Guns | 1 in the bow, two decks of 8 a side, 2 in the stern | 4 in the bow, two decks of 12 a side, 2 in the stern |
| Top speed | 96 km/h | 85 km/h |
| A full circle | 31 s | 39 s |
| Climbing | 11 m a second | 7 m a second |
| Hull | 8,800 | 14,700 |
| Crystals | 3,520 | 5,460 |
| A broadside reloads in | 2.1 s | 2 s |

**Yours are better found than a raider's**, so buying one feels mighty at once (October 9: a new Galleon is well ahead of a Frigate with every upgrade, and the Man-o'-war ahead again; see [How hard it is](#how-hard-it-is)), and she's still fun to fly: your crew turn both two fifths quicker than a raider crew does (a full circle in 31 and 39 seconds, not 44 and 54), and climb them 15% and 40% quicker; your shipwrights plate their hulls far heavier and your crystal-cutters set their crystals deeper (2.2 times a raider's on the Galleon, 2.1 times on the Man-o'-war: a raider's hull is 4,000 and 7,000, her crystals 1,600 and 2,600; a big ship is a big target, her crystals heaped high on her deck, and every raider's shots find her); and your gunners, the best in the sky, reload their heavy guns quicker than any other crew, a fifth quicker on the Galleon and a quarter on the Man-o'-war (a broadside in 2.1 and 2 seconds, where a Frigate's takes 2.6; a raider crew takes 15% and 30% longer than a Frigate's). Both decks fire together down the side, so a Galleon's broadside is 16 guns and a Man-o'-war's 24, rippling bow to stern in about half a second, with the heel, the kick and the roll of thunder of a big ship (a Man-o'-war's rolls on twice). The guns' label says "Port broadside · 24 guns".

**The view stands further back** from a big ship (about 105 m behind a Galleon and 160 m behind a Man-o'-war), so all of her is on the screen, her stern too, and raiders coming in show clear above her masts.

**Your progress is saved** (Crystal Shards, ships, upgrades, skies and best waves): in the browser on that device, and, on claude.ai, in the game page's own private store for you, so it follows you to another device. Nobody else can see it.

Playing on your phone and your laptop, **the newest progress always wins**. A game left open on one device picks up what you did on the other (the port says "Your progress from your other device is here"; a new browser just shows it), and it checks again just before it saves, so it never writes its older progress over newer. **Shards won on a voyage are never lost that way**: if the other device saved something this one hadn't heard about yet, the voyage's shards are added to the newer progress, and the port says so. A save that can't get through (the connection blinks, or there's none) goes up a moment later, or the next time the game is open and online on that device. What can still be lost is a change made in port on a device that hadn't caught up yet: a ship or an upgrade bought, the skies or the crystal power. The newer progress replaces it, and any shards it cost come back.

## A voyage

- **The waves.** The first comes a few seconds after you set sail, 1.5 to 1.9 km ahead of you, more or less. A banner says what's coming, where she is from you in left and right ("dead ahead", "ahead on your right", "off your left side", "behind you on your right", "behind you"), and the wind ("the wind behind you", "a head wind", "a wind from your left").
- **Crystal Shards.** A downed raider spills its shards as glowing amber crystals that drift slowly down, with a gold flash, and her bounty rises out of the wreck in gold: "◆ 75", or "Captain's bounty" or "Treasure" over a big one (just under a banner, if one is showing there, so both can be read, and never on the card between waves: if the card is coming up where the bounty would rise, it shows just under the card instead, or just above it where the card is at the bottom). Fly within about 140 m and they're drawn to your ship, each with a little gold glint, and the shard count in the corner pops gold and counts up as they come in; leave them 30 seconds and they're gone. Skiffs are worth ◆ 15, Cutters 30, Brigs 60 and Frigates 100, and a raider captain four times as much. Each wave adds 10% (the fifth wave's raiders are worth 40% more than the first's), the skies add their share, and beating a wave adds ◆ 20 for each wave so far.
- **After each wave.** When the last raider of a wave goes down, the world slows to a quarter speed for about a second and a half, so you can watch her go; then a card rises into view showing what you earned (it counts up), what's in the hold this voyage, and **what the next wave is**. It's a strip docked at the bottom of the screen (on a phone held upright, just above the buttons; held sideways, at the top under the compass, as the bottom is for your thumbs), so it never covers the wreck and her shards in the middle. **Sail on** (it sails on by itself after 25 seconds) or go **Back to port** and keep it all. While it's up, the score in the corner shows the wave you just beat, as the card does. On a laptop with the mouse locked to the view you can't click it, so **Enter** sails on and **B** goes back to port, and the buttons say so. Meanwhile the crew patch her up, back to full in about 8 seconds: the fires go out, and her holes are patched (see [Battle scars](#battle-scars)). Every voyage from port starts afresh at wave 1.
- **Going down.** If your ship goes down, the crew get her home with half this voyage's shards. Pausing and going back to port in the middle of a fight also keeps half; between waves it keeps all.
- **The wind** changes with every wave (in a storm it blows harder, from the storm, in gusts). It's shown next to the compass in words: "Wind behind: 8% faster", "Head wind: 8% slower" or "Wind across: no help" (on a phone just the arrow and "8% faster"), up to 14%. The arrow points the way it blows (up is the way you're heading). Raiders feel it too.
- **The region's name** shows in the sky when you cross into a new part of Aethermoor, with a line under it about its air ("Hot, dusty air"), but only at a quiet moment: never in the middle of a fight, never over a banner. If you cross over during a fight, it shows once the wave is beaten. Each region's air comes in gently over a few seconds as you cross (see [Storms, clouds and the sky](#storms-clouds-and-the-sky)).
- **The sun** is low in the west, a late afternoon all voyage long, and it throws the shadows of your masts, sails and rigging across your deck. Only a storm darkens it.
- **The Surge.** **R**, or the Surge button on a phone: the crystals pour into the sails for 3 seconds, 60% more top speed, then 15 seconds to build up again (the bar in the top-left panel). For running from a broadside, catching a fleeing raider, or reaching shards before they fall. How it looks: see [The Surge](#the-surge).
- **Raider captains** lead every fifth wave: wave 5 is a captain's Brig. A captain's ship looks the leader: black sails edged in crimson, blackened iron fittings, crimson crystals, red eyes glowing at her bow, gold pennants and a great black-and-crimson banner, and a gold tag. It's twice as sturdy, hits 20% harder and reloads 10% faster, and it's worth four times the shards. On Fair Winds, where a new Captain learns the ropes, she's no tougher than her crew, hits no harder and reloads no faster, but she's still worth four times the shards. (A Man-o'-war needs no captain, and a treasure ship runs rather than leads: in a wave with them, the captain sails the next biggest ship. Only a wave with nothing else to sail has a Galleon captain, and she still runs.) Her ship is made ready while the card before her wave is up, so her wave arrives without a stutter.
- **Treasure ships.** A treasure ship is a rich prize, her hold worth ◆ 300 before the bonuses, whatever she sails. **Until you own a Galleon she's a treasure Brig**: a rich merchant Brig laden with shards (Chris: giant raiders only once you own one); once you own the Galleon, treasure Galleons come instead. She looks it: wine-red sails edged in gold, gilded fittings that glint and twinkle, open chests of gold on her deck, a glittering gold wake, and her hull sprays gold where you hit it. She sails across your path until you come within a kilometre or hit her, then runs, weaving, covering her escape with her stern guns; laden with her treasure, she sails at 85% of her class's top speed, so even a Skiff can run her down. Your guns lock on to her sails rather than her hull: **shoot her sails away (or her hull down to a quarter) and she strikes her colours**: she doesn't blow up, but lowers her pennants, glints gold from her hold, and settles away below the clouds, spilling her shards. Let her get 3.6 km away and she escapes with her treasure. Don't pull alongside a treasure Galleon: two decks of eight guns a side (a treasure Brig has six). Any ship can sail as a treasure ship, in the same colours and running the same way, and her wave is announced as a treasure ship's ("Wave 6: a treasure ship", "a treasure Brig and a Cutter"; two of them, "two treasure ships"). A Galleon that leads a raider captain's wave wears the captain's colours, but she still runs like any Galleon.
- **The Man-o'-war** sails among the raiders only once you own one (until then a raider captain's Frigate leads her waves in her place). She's a fortress: hull 7,000, two decks of twelve guns a side, four in the bow. Her broadside can wreck a Frigate in a few volleys, but she turns slowly and barely climbs, so stay off her beam: above or below her, or off her bow or stern. From above, her five crystal columns are open to your guns; sink her crystals and she's out of the fight. They're her weak points, and they look it: they glow brighter than any other ship's and beat slowly like a heart, and lock on to her and their edges glow gold. Each one that gives out blows out in a shower of gold and violet sparks, goes dark and smokes, and she dips at that end.

### Phone

- **Left thumb**: put it down anywhere on the left side and a stick appears under it. Push left or right to turn, up to climb, down to dive.
- **Right thumb**: drag anywhere on the right side to swing the camera round the ship and aim. While your thumb rests there the view stays where you aimed it; lift it, and after a few seconds the view eases back behind the ship.
- **Fire**: hold to keep firing as the guns reload, and **slide your thumb off it to aim while you fire**. **Surge**: the round blue button beside it. Above them, which guns will fire, and their reload.
- **The shot button**: once you have chain shot or crystal breakers, a round button over Surge shows the shot loaded (its picture, its name and its colour; the breakers with how many volleys are left). Tap it to load the next kind (see [Tactics](#tactics)).
- **Patch**: once she's hurt (her worst part under 70%), a green round button shows left of the sail buttons. Tap it and the crew patch her up; it glows while they work, then a ring round it fills as they get ready again.
- **Sail − / Sail +**: hold to take in or let out sail.
- **The pause button** (two bars, under the compass; held sideways, beside the map) pauses. The pause card has **How to fly**, which shows all the touch controls, and **Settings**.
- **The corner map**: tap it to make it big. The big map fits the screen over a dimmed sky and names the regions; tap the cross, the map or the sky round it to close it.
- **The gear** (on the title screen and in port, and on the pause card as **Settings**) opens the Settings card (see [Settings](#settings)). **Fire button on the left** there swaps the sides for left-handed players: Fire, Surge and Sail on the left, the stick under your right thumb, and aiming on the left. The hint and How to fly say it that way round too.
- A short hint in the lower corner says how to steer, aim and fire on your first three voyages; it fades a few seconds after you first touch the screen.

On a phone the game draws a little less sharply so it stays smooth (the picture setting Balanced, with fewer sparks and puffs of smoke), and pauses by itself if you leave the page. If the phone clears the game's pictures while you're away (phones do, after a long while), the game draws them again when you come back.

### The screen

Everything on the screen has its own place, on a laptop, a phone held upright and a phone held sideways (or any short window), so nothing lands on anything else (the check measures it at three laptop sizes and six phone sizes, down to the smallest phone held sideways with the browser's bars showing, 568 by 320):

- **Along the top**: your ship's panel (left), the compass and wind (middle), the pause button, the corner map and the score (right). On a phone the ship's panel is smaller: speed and height on one line, then the sail, then the bars.
- **Under the compass**: the region's name or a banner (they take turns: a banner always wins), with a soft dark backing so they read over bright cloud. On a narrow phone held sideways (667 by 375 and smaller) the compass is on one line and the banner's words a little smaller, so "Your first voyage, Captain" fits on one line under it; on the smallest (568 by 320) the banner sits just under the pause button, as wide as the room between your ship's panel and the map, and so does the card between waves (the crosshair makes way for it then, as there's nothing to fire at); the toast and a warning are a little wider there, and the raiders' tags a little smaller, so a crowd of them all round still finds room.
- **Hidden in the cloud**: while your ship is hidden deep in cloud in a fight, "Hidden in the cloud" shows calmly in the warning's place under the crosshair (a real warning takes its place).
- **The toast** (a raider down, a Surge) has its own place: on a laptop under the guns' label, on a phone held upright under the banner, held sideways under the crosshair. A **warning** (sinking, the Thinning, the open sea) shows just under the crosshair (held sideways, under the toast). On a laptop window that isn't so tall (a 1366 by 768 laptop's), the banner's words are a little smaller, so it fits between the toast and the crosshair.
- **Your own ship stays clear.** The warning never lies across your ship: where she is under the crosshair (seen from behind, she nearly always is), it moves just under her keel, or just over her mast heads, or just over the crosshair, wherever there's room clear of the panels, the toast and a banner. Raiders' tags keep off her too: one at the bottom edge for a raider behind you goes just beside her, and tags keep off the warning, so a battered ship seen from behind shows her scars, and every tag and the warning can still be read. (Her shape on the screen is taken as the box round her, in four slabs from stern to bow, each as it shows in perspective; worked out each frame from a few numbers, so it costs a phone nothing.)
- **At the bottom**: on a phone, the touch buttons (right, or left if you choose) and the hint (the other side); on a laptop, the keys. With the shot button over Surge the guns' label sits a little higher; on a phone held sideways Surge and the shot button are a little smaller (on the smallest, Fire and the sail buttons too), so the buttons are no taller than without it, and the Patch button sits left of the sail buttons, clear of the warning (a little narrower there).
- **Raiders' tags** at the edge of the screen keep clear of the panels along the top (the guns' label too) and the buttons along the bottom, arrows and all. A tag flashing "Broadside!" (over her or at the edge) says it in place of her bars, so it's hardly taller, and its bigger arrow is given room too. Where there's no room for a tag between the panels at the edge (down the right of a phone held sideways), it slides in towards the middle. Tags at the edge never land on each other: where the panels leave room down the side for only a tag or two (a phone held sideways, with raiders behind you), the others go just beside them.
- **The big map's names** each keep clear of the others: where two regions' names would meet (the Verdant Wilds and the Hearthsea, on a phone), one moves down out of the way.
- **The pause card** (and the card when your ship goes down) lies over everything, even the card between waves; pausing closes the big map.

## Settings

The gear on the title screen and in port, or **Settings** on the pause card, opens the Settings card. Everything on it takes effect at once and is kept on that device only (in the browser, never in the progress that goes between devices), so a phone and a laptop can each have their own:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Sound on**, **Music**, **Sounds** | See [Sound and music](#sound-and-music) |
| **Aim speed** | How far the view swings for the same drag or mouse move: Slow (half as far), Gentle, Normal, Quick, Fast (1.8 times as far) |
| **Up and down** | Normal, or Flipped (pushing up looks down) |
| **Picture** | **Smooth**, **Balanced** or **Sharp**. Smooth runs best on older phones: one screen pixel to each of the page's, small sun shadows, half the cloud puffs, raiders switching to their far model sooner, fewer sparks, smoke and debris, half the rain, no scraps of scud, no sun's rays (on the title's sunset) and no glitter on the sea. Sharp looks finest. A phone starts on Balanced, a laptop on Sharp. |
| **Camera shake** | Off, the view holds still: no shaking when you're hit and no kick from your guns |
| **Fire button on the left** | Phones only: swaps the touch buttons and the stick for left-handed players |

| Picture | Sharpness (screen pixels to the page's) | Sun shadows | Cloud puffs | Sparks, smoke, debris (of a laptop's full count): phone / laptop | Rain, scud, sun's rays, glitter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smooth | up to 1 | 512 | 55 | 45% / 50% | half the rain; no scud, rays or glitter |
| Balanced | up to 1.5 | 1,024 | 85 | 60% / 75% | all |
| Sharp | up to 2 | 2,048 | 110 | 60% / 100% (a phone never goes past its 60%: its batches are made that size) | all |

The cloud deck's pattern is worked out once, when the game starts, and kept as a picture; the deck and the cloud shadows on the ground read it from there. That saves a phone most of the work of drawing the sky, and looks the same.

## Aiming and the guns

**Where you look decides which guns fire.** Look ahead and the bow guns fire, look to the left (port) or right (starboard) and that side's broadside fires, look back and the stern guns fire. The name of the guns that will fire is shown under the compass (on a phone, above the Fire button), with a bar that fills as they reload.

- Every gun fires from where it really sits on the model, so a broadside comes out of the gun ports along the side.
- **A broadside ripples down the side**, bow to stern: a gun every 55 thousandths of a second, the whole side in about half a second (on the Galleon and the Man-o'-war, both decks fire together at each port). Bow and stern guns fire in pairs, a moment apart. The first gun fires the moment you press, and the reload starts then.
- Guns only swing a little, and tilt even less, so you have to put the ship where its guns can reach. Flying well above or below a ship keeps it out of your broadside, and keeps you out of its.
- The shots are like crystal comets that take time to fly: a hot head and a long tail fading out behind, heavier for a broadside than a chaser, gold for the Captain's and red for the raiders'. A shot that hits nothing burns out in a little wisp of sparks.
- **Point the crosshair at a raider and the guns lock on.** The crosshair turns red and the raider's tag glows. Locked on, your gunners aim ahead of the raider, where it will be when the shot gets there, and at the middle of its hull. If the guns facing it can't reach it (too far, or too far above or below), the crosshair fades and the gun label says "out of reach".

| Guns | Speed of the shot | Reload | Reach | Swing / tilt | Shot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chasers** (bow and stern, and the Skiff's swivels) | 430 m/s | 1.1 s | about 1.4 km | 35° / 15° | 28 |
| **Broadsides** (the guns along the sides) | 320 m/s | 2.6 s (the side ripples off in about half a second) | about 830 m | 43° / 9° | 55, spreading a little |

**Bigger ships carry bigger guns**: a shot's weight is scaled by the ship's class: 0.8 on a Skiff, 0.9 on a Cutter, 1 on a Brig, 1.1 on a Frigate, 1.15 on a Galleon and 1.25 on a Man-o'-war (and then by crystal power and, for raiders, the skies). A raider Galleon's and Man-o'-war's heavy guns take their crews 15% and 30% longer to reload, and on your own big two your gunners reload a fifth and a quarter quicker than any crew (a broadside in 2.1 and 2 s). Each hit takes its weight off whatever it hits: the hull, the sails or the crystals.

## Tactics

Ways to fight smarter than trading broadsides (October 9; the rules are in `src/game/tactics.js`). Each has a key on a laptop and a button on a phone.

### Shot

Your guns can load three kinds of shot (Chris chose all three):

| Shot | What it's for | On the hull | On the sails | On the crystals | How it flies | Loading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Round shot** (gold) | all-round work; always aboard | as usual | as usual | as usual | as usual | as usual |
| **Chain shot** (lilac, two heads spinning round each other) | tearing sails: a ship with none crawls and turns badly, and a treasure ship gives up | a quarter | two and a half times | a quarter | a little slower, and about two thirds as far | as usual |
| **Crystal breakers** (pale blue) | cracking crystals, to bring a big ship down from above | half | under a third | 1.6 times | as usual | a quarter longer; **five volleys a voyage** |

- **On a laptop** 1, 2 or 3 loads round shot, chain shot or crystal breakers, and a right-click loads the next one you have. **On a phone** the shot button over Surge (it shows once you have chain shot or breakers) shows what's loaded; tap it for the next.
- **Changing shot means reloading**: every battery draws the old shot and loads the new, so pick your moment. A toast says "Loading chain shot".
- **Your gunners aim for what the shot is for**: with round shot her hull (a treasure ship's fore sails), with chain shot the middle of her sails, with breakers the top of her crystals (her hull, once that part's gone). They lead her by the shot's own speed, and "out of reach" allows for chain shot's shorter reach.
- **The guns' label says what's loaded** on a laptop ("Port broadside · 6 guns · Chain shot"; the breakers with how many volleys are left), and the reload bar takes the shot's colour. When the last volley of breakers goes, round shot is loaded again by itself ("Out of crystal breakers").
- **Getting them**: chain shot is yours the first time a treasure ship strikes her colours to you (a banner: "Chain shot! Her crew left chain shot in the hold: press 2"), crystal breakers the first time you bring down a raider captain's Frigate. Or buy them in port, under **Shot** on any ship you own (chain shot ◆ 200, crystal breakers ◆ 600): bought or earned once, they're yours for every ship. A sea trial earns nothing.
- **Sails are thin across a ship**: chain shot does best at her bow or her stern, where all her sails face you (chasing a runner, say). From her side many shots pass between her masts.

### Dodging a broadside: the red fan

- Just before a raider fires her side at you, her gun ports glow (red brightening to gold), **a soft red fan opens off her side** showing where her guns can reach, with bands of red rippling out of it faster as she's about to fire, and her tag flashes **"Broadside!"** with a ring closing beside it (over her, or at the edge of the screen). Inside her fan the red bands sweep past you.
- **Her gunners aim where you're heading**: they lay their guns as the warning starts, on the spot you'll be at if you hold your course. **Climb, dive or turn hard, and the whole side goes where you would have been.** Hold your course and it hits as hard as ever. Climbing is safest: dive, and her shots may find your sails.
- **How long the warning lasts** depends on the skies: 1 second on Fair Winds, 0.8 on Crosswinds, 0.6 on the Maelstrom (and 0.15 s longer from a Galleon or a Man-o'-war). Raiders' broadside crews reload a fifth quicker than they used to, so they fire their sides as often as before (about every 3.9 s on Crosswinds).
- Bow and stern guns, and the Skiff's light swivels, still fire without a warning.
- **What it costs a phone**: each fan is one shape of about 170 triangles shared by every raider, drawn in one go, only while she readies her side (most of a second in every four), with its glow worked out on the graphics card from a few numbers.

### Raking fire

- **A broadside's shot flying down a ship's length** (within 25 degrees of her bow or her stern) **does half as much again.** Cross ahead of her bow or behind her stern and fire your side down her length: the hits burst bigger and **"Raked!"** shows beside the crosshair.
- **It works both ways**: don't let a raider cross your bow. The first time one rakes you in a voyage a toast says "Raked! Don't let them cross your bow", and the red at the screen's edge flares harder.
- Only broadsides rake: a Skiff's bow guns chasing you don't.

### Patching her up

- **X on a laptop, or the Patch button on a phone** (green, left of the sail buttons, once her worst part is under 70%): the crew patch whatever's worst off (the hull, the sails or the crystals) **by a third, over 6 seconds**, and that row in your panel glows green.
- **While they work, your guns reload at half speed.** Then they need 30 seconds before they can patch again: on a laptop the Patch row in your panel fills up, on a phone a ring fills round the button. Pressed too soon, a toast says how long ("The crew can patch her again in 12 s").
- Between waves the crew still patch her back to full for nothing.

### Smarter raiders

- **Crosswinds**: a Brig, Frigate or Man-o'-war you point your bow at sails for a spot just ahead of it, to cross your bow and rake you.
- **The Maelstrom**: that, and Skiffs and Cutters make their attack runs at your stern, where your guns are few, and a raider your guns are locked on to, with your loaded broadside pointed at her, may suddenly climb or dive out of it. (Small raiders at your stern on Crosswinds too made it much harder for a Captain who doesn't use the tactics, so they're the Maelstrom's.)
- **Fair Winds**: they fight simply.

## How a fight feels

- **Your guns.** Each port spits a tongue of flame and a jet of white-grey powder smoke that billows out a dozen metres or more, so a broadside leaves a thick bank of smoke along her side that rolls back as she sails on and thins away over five seconds (you see it from behind your ship too, over her far side). The ship heels away from the side that fired. Each gun that fires shoves the view back a little and widens it for a moment, so a broadside rolls like thunder under your feet. On an Android phone your volleys buzz the phone too, a pulse a gun.
- **Your hits.** When your shot lands, four little ticks flash round the crosshair in the colour of what it hit: gold for the hull, cream for the sails, orange with a sparkle for the crystals (the same colours as the raider's bars). A rippling broadside's hits read as a chain of flickers. A shot that brings a raider down flashes a red X and a ring. On the raider's tag, the bar for the part you hit flashes, and the piece you knocked off shows white for a moment before it drains away.
- **What a hit knocks off.** Each hit throws sparks the colour of what it hit, and debris you can read at a glance: splinters of wood that tumble and fall, with a puff of dust, from the hull; torn scraps of canvas that flap and flutter slowly down from the sails, in the sail's own colour (rust-red from a raider, black from a raider captain, cream from yours), each with a ragged edge and catching the light on its folds; glittering amber shards from the crystals. Shoot a treasure ship's sails and you can see her canvas coming away. Hits on your own ship throw your own splinters and canvas.
- **Hits on you.** The view jolts, hardest for a hit on the hull, a rattle for the crystals and a little for the sails, and it lurches towards the side that was hit. A red arc glows on a ring round the middle of the screen, pointing to where the shot came from, and the red at the edge of the screen is strongest on that side. The row for what was hit (Hull, Sails or Crystals) flashes in your panel, and its bar shows the chunk knocked off. On an Android phone each hit buzzes the phone, and bringing a raider down gives a longer buzz (but not a treasure ship giving up: she goes quietly). An iPhone can't buzz from a web page.
- **Near misses.** A raider's shot that only just misses flares bright as it streaks past, with a flash of sparks and a twitch of the view.
- **Blasts and the Surge.** A raider blowing up within 250 m shakes the view (each blast of her chain, too), and so does starting a Surge.
- **Slow motion.** Bringing down the last raider of a wave slows the world to a quarter speed for about a second and a half, the view narrowing a little; then it speeds back up and the card between waves rises. When your own ship goes down, the world slows to half speed for a moment.
- **A raider's broadside** is coming when her gun ports glow red along her side and a red fan opens off it: for the skies' warning time (0.8 s on Crosswinds, a little longer on the Galleon and the Man-o'-war) they glow, brightening to hot gold, and if her lids on that side are shut they fly open and her guns run out, then her side ripples off. Her tag flashes red and says "Broadside!", with a ring beside it closing as she's about to fire, over her or at the edge of the screen (where its arrow flashes too: on a phone a raider alongside is usually off the screen). Her gunners aim where you're heading, so that's your moment to climb, dive or turn (see [Tactics](#tactics)).
- **Less motion.** If your phone or laptop is set to reduce motion, the shake and kick are cut to a third, and the phone doesn't buzz.
- **On a phone** the game uses fewer sparks, puffs of smoke and pieces of debris (six in ten), and draws them no bigger than a phone can fill quickly, so a big fight stays smooth. Your gunsmoke is one puff a port instead of two, but a much bigger one that rises a little and lasts longer (seven seconds), so a broadside still leaves a thick bank of smoke along her side, showing over her deck. Masts don't fall on a phone (the sails burn away instead).

## Battle scars

Every ship shows what the fight has done to her, your own and every raider alike, and you can read how hurt a raider is just by looking at her.

- **Hits leave their mark where they land.** A shot into the hull or the deck leaves a black hole ringed with pale splintered wood and blackened with soot, glowing with embers for a few seconds before it smoulders. A broadside's shot leaves a bigger scar than a chaser's, and shots landing close together (within about 3 m, more on the Galleon and the Man-o'-war) make one bigger scar. A ship keeps up to a dozen scars, so a long fight's first hits keep their marks; only on a ship already scarred all over does a hit far from the rest take the place of a patch, or of an old cool scar. A shot through the sails tears a ragged hole with scorched brown edges, right where it went through the canvas, in that sail alone (not the one across the mast).
- **Torn sails fray.** As the sails bar drops below about 60%, the sails' loose edges fray into tatters and the canvas flaps harder; with no sails left they're rags. On a raider far off, you can see she's lost her sails from her outline.
- **The hull gets sooty** and streaked as the hull bar drops, and a badly holed ship **leans over** towards the side that took the most hits (your own a little less, so your guns still reach).
- **Crystals dim where they're hit.** Each cluster of crystals dims as it takes damage, white cracks spread across it, the furnace windows under it go darker, its glow fades and fewer sparks drift off it; on your own ship its lamp dims too. Below a third on the crystals bar, all of the ship's crystals sputter and flicker together. A ship going down has her crystals fade out over two seconds.
- **Smoke comes from where she was hit.** A fresh hit smoulders with a thin wisp of smoke for a few seconds. Below half her hull a ship trails smoke from her open scars (not from her patches), thicker and darker as the hull goes.
- **Below a quarter of her hull she burns.** Flames burst from her two worst wounds (three below an eighth), flickering and streaming back along her as she flies, the planks round them glowing. They're big (nearly a quarter of her length tall: some 10 m on a Frigate) and bright against the sky as well as her dark hull, with an orange glow round each, and they grow a little more far off, so a burning raider across the fight reads as burning. Her smoke pours from the flames' tips and climbs fast, standing up over her sails, so even your own ship seen from behind shows she's on fire. A ship going down holed burns along all her scars.
- **Her wounds read from afar.** The further off a ship is (from about 30 m to 110 m from the camera), the bigger her open holes are drawn (over half as big again) and the darker their soot, so a raider across a fight, or your own ship from behind, shows her holes clearly. Close up they're their true size.
- **Between waves your crew patch her up.** As her bars fill, the fires go out and the embers cool; each hole in her planks becomes a square of fresh planks, its soot fading and the patch shrinking as she mends (to half its size, and it never grows back, even when she's hit again next wave); each hole in her sails is sewn up and covered with a darker square of new canvas with a stitched edge; the cracks in her crystals fade. Raiders never patch theirs.
- **In port she's as good as new**, and every voyage starts with a clean ship.
- **It costs a phone almost nothing.** The scars are painted on the ships' own surfaces as they're drawn: no new shapes and no extra drawing, at every level of detail, and every ship of a class shares the same work. A ship without a scar skips the scar painting altogether (sails with no holes or fraying, crystals with no cracks), and one with scars pays only for the ones she has. All the flames in the sky are drawn in one go (at most 16 on a phone, 28 on a laptop).
- **See them in the ships demo**: the New, Battered and Wrecked buttons show any ship after a fight, at any detail. On a phone held sideways its buttons sit on three short lines along the bottom (each slides sideways if it doesn't fit), and the stats start folded away (**Stats** brings them out), so the ship shows above them: the view stands far enough back that all of her (keel, ram tip and mast heads) stays in sight as she turns, from the moment the page opens. Tapping a ship, All six, a detail level or colours answers at once.

## Ships that move like they're alive

Every ship moves as she's handled, your own and every raider alike:

- **Her wings fold and spread with her sails.** Take in sail (S on a laptop, Sail − on a phone) and her wing sails sweep back along the hull like a bird folding its wings, their tips dipping; set more sail and they spread wide again. A Surge snaps them right open with a shiver. Raiders spread theirs to chase and half fold them as they slow to bring a broadside round. A ship going down folds hers. Shots still hit the sails wherever the wings are, and a hole stays where it struck as she spreads them again.
- **Her pennants** stream out straight behind the mast at speed and hang limp when she's slow.
- **Her gun crews.** Between waves your gun-port lids are shut and your guns run in. As a wave arrives the lids fly open, bow first, and each gun runs out once its lid is up; the card between waves closes them again. Fire a side between waves and its lids burst open with its first gun, the guns already run out, so no shot leaves through a shut lid. When a side fires, each gun kicks back in at its own turn as the broadside ripples down the side, stays in while the crew reload, and runs out again just as the reload bar fills. Your bow and stern guns kick back along their barrels. A raider's lids fly open on the side she's about to fire, as her ports glow, and stay open while she's in the fight.
- **A wake of Aether.** A soft, shimmering ribbon of stirred-up Aether streams behind every ship from under her stern, longer and brighter the faster she flies: a soft gold shimmer behind yours (fainter and a little narrower than a raider's, as you look straight down it all the time, never a hard bright band), red behind a raider, crimson behind a raider captain, glittering gold behind a treasure ship, broad and orange behind a Man-o'-war. Far off, a raider's wake grows longer (up to three times as long at about 1.3 km), wider and brighter, and stops shimmering, so even a raider that's only a speck trails one clear streak (not a row of dots) that shows which way she's going. A raider lost in cloud leaves no wake to give her away: hers fades out, and comes back when she shows herself. In hard turns, steep dives and a Surge, thin white vapour trails peel off your wingtips.
- **Raiders in their colours**: a crew's rust-red sails; a raider captain's black sails edged in crimson, blackened iron, crimson crystals, red eyes and banner; a treasure ship's wine-red and gold, glinting, with chests of gold on her deck. Any ship can sail as a treasure ship. See them on any ship in the ships demo: the **Yours**, **Raider**, **Captain** and **Treasure** buttons; **Sails set** and **Sails in** fold her wings; **Ports shut**, **Guns out** and **Fire!** work her guns.
- **The Man-o'-war's weak points**: her crystal columns beat like a heart, glow gold at their edges while your guns are locked on her, and blow out one by one (see [The raiders](#the-raiders)).
- **What it costs a phone.** All of it is worked out on the graphics card from a few numbers each ship keeps, at every level of detail: no new shapes. The guns, lids and spikes that move are drawn in one more go for each ship close enough to be drawn in detail (none far off), and every wake in the sky is drawn in one go. A ship in any colours needs no new drawing work, so a captain's or a treasure ship's wave arrives without a stutter. Far off, a ship's yards lose the little spikes at their tips (too small to see), and in the middle distance her gun ports have plain lids.

## Storms, clouds and the sky

The voyages keep their late-afternoon light all the way (Chris chose storms and flying through clouds, and no day turning to night). What changes the sky:

- **Storms.** Some waves bring a storm: never on Fair Winds, waves 8 and 13 on Crosswinds, waves 4, 8 and 12 on the Maelstrom (and two waves in five after the fifteenth). While the card before a storm wave is up, it says "A storm is rolling in from the west" (or wherever it's coming from), and that side of the sky goes slate dark, with a wall of storm cloud on the horizon: heaped billows, most about 9 degrees high, every crown lumpy with smaller billows like a cauliflower, lit pale on their tops and shoulders (warmer towards the sun), falling into dark slate at their foot with shafts of rain hanging under them, slowly swelling, drifting and churning upward; at the storm's edges the wall breaks up into separate heaps, and as it comes they rise one by one. On a phone held upright, with its narrower view, the billows are smaller, so several always show across it. And a low rumble far off. As the wave arrives the storm rolls in over 20 seconds: the sky darkens to slate, the cloud floor thickens and goes grey, the light dims, the haze closes in, rain streaks slant past the ship (more slanted the faster she flies), and the wind blows harder, from the storm (16% to 24% of top speed, in gusts that swell for a couple of seconds, heel the ship a little and shake the view). Every 4 to 12 seconds lightning forks down into the clouds a few kilometres off (mostly where you're looking), with a violet-white flash that lights up the sky, the clouds and the ships, and its thunder a moment later. Beat the wave and the storm clears over 20 seconds. A storm is for the look and the wind: lightning never strikes a ship.
- **Big clouds you fly into.** A voyage always starts in clear air. Fly into one of the big clouds and the view fills with soft white mist streaming past (wisps rushing outward as she flies on, sliding as you swing the view); your ship shows through it but the world outside fades into the mist a few hundred metres off. Out the other side it clears in a breath. The cloud floor is the same: dive into it and you fly through a layer of mist, and under it is a grey ceiling with the map below.
- **Scud.** Near the cloud floor, round the big clouds, in a storm and in the mist, small scraps of cloud drift past close by, so you feel your speed.
- **Cloud banks.** Every third wave, and every storm wave, comes out of a bank of six big clouds lying across the raiders' way in, about 500 to 800 m ahead of them, so their red sails loom out of the cloud. The bank fades away once the wave is beaten.
- **Raiders aim worse at you in a cloud** (Chris asked for that): their shots spread two and a half times as wide while you're inside one, so only about a third as many land (the check counts them, with the same luck in and out of a cloud).
- **Hiding.** Deep in cloud you're hidden: inside a big cloud, or in thick cloud floor, anywhere from just under its top (your masts still showing above it) to about 40 m under it. Then far-off raiders lose you (see [The raiders](#the-raiders)), and "Hidden in the cloud" shows under the crosshair. Firing your guns gives you away for 4 seconds. Raiders can hide there too: one lost in cloud far off can't be locked on to, her wake and the glow of her fires fade out, and her tag waits where you last saw her, saying "Lost in the cloud" and how far off that spot is (a ring on the map). As she readies a broadside, her glowing gun ports give her away: her tag jumps to her, and you can lock on to her until she's fired. Hiding is a place to break off, wait for a raider to come looking, and catch her as she does.
- **Towering clouds round the horizon**: a skyline of big billowing clouds stands all round the far horizon, lit gold-white on the sun's side and shaded blue-grey away from it, so the sky has depth. A storm on its way stands in front of them as its own billowing wall (see Storms above).
- **Your shadow and the glory.** Over the cloud floor, your ship's shadow lies on the cloud below, cast along the sun, softer the higher you fly; look down and away from the sun and a small rainbow ring glows round it (the "glory" pilots see from planes), with a faint white cloud-bow further out. It goes in a storm.
- **The sea glitters** where the sun's reflection would be: a path of sparkles on the painted sea, out of the clouds' shadows (the land never does).
- **The sun's glare.** Look up into the sun and a soft glare blooms round it, with a few faint coloured rings along the line through the middle of the view. A big cloud in front of the sun, the mist or a storm takes it away.
- **Each region's air**, blended in gently over a few seconds as you cross into it: **the Sunscorch Wastes** warm and hazy, with dust drifting by ("Hot, dusty air"); **the Ironspire Peaks** cold and clear, with snow on the wind ("Cold, clear air, and snow on the wind"); **the Gloomfen** low, green-grey mist and pale motes ("Low, damp mist"); **the Gloamwood** dusky violet ("Dusky, violet air"); **the Hearthsea** clear and bright ("Clear, bright air over the water"); **the Verdant Wilds** soft and green ("Soft, green air over the forest"); **the Open Sea** deep blue ("Deep blue sky and a fresh wind"). The line shows under the region's name.
- **What it costs a phone.** The storm's colours, the region's air, the front, the flash, the glory and the mist are numbers every part of the sky already reads, so they cost no extra drawing; the towering clouds read their skyline from one small picture made as the game starts. Rain (350 streaks on a phone, 900 on a laptop) is one draw, so is a bolt of lightning (for a quarter of a second), the mist (only inside a cloud), the scud (only near clouds) and the glare (only looking into the sun). The check times a phone-sized frame: the sky's new work costs about 2% of a clear frame (in software drawing; a phone's graphics card skips what isn't showing), and a storm or a cloud a little more (a storm, a cloud or a bank of cloud may cost at most a fifth more than clear sky; they're timed in turns with clear sky, six times over, and the middle of the six turns counts, so other work on the machine slows both sides alike). In a battle with three raiders on a phone-sized screen, a frame in a storm costs the same as one in clear sky, within 1% (measured on October 7, held upright and sideways). The Settings card's **Smooth** picture halves the rain and leaves out the scud, the sun's rays and the sea's glitter.

## Wrecks

A raider going down is worth watching, and how she goes depends on what brought her down:

- **Her hull gone: she blows apart.** A white flash and a great burst of fire, splinters and canvas. Then a chain of blasts walks along her hull from stern to bow: three on a Skiff, four on a Brig, five on a Frigate, nine on a Man-o'-war. Her crystals sputter and go dark, and her sails flare up and burn away. She rolls right over and falls nose-down, trailing a thick column of smoke and fire that hangs in the sky after her: one broad, rolling column, its puffs all different sizes and shades, black and thick near her and lit orange from below near the flames, greying as it thins away above.
- **Her masts fall** (on a laptop, when she's close enough to be drawn in detail): a moment after she blows, her masts crack at the deck one after another, topple over the side with their sails, break away and tumble down trailing rolling smoke, all the way through the clouds. Once she's far enough away to be drawn plainly, she shows no masts either.
- **Her crystals dead: she sinks.** No blasts: her crystals sputter and shatter into amber shards, they go dark, and she sinks upright in grey smoke.
- **A treasure ship striking her colours** doesn't blow up: her pennants come down their masts, gold glints from her hold, and she settles away.
- **Every wreck goes below the clouds.** A sinking ship (crystals dead, or colours struck) sinks slowly at first; a few seconds later the last of her lift gives out and she drops away faster and faster, so from a fight's usual height she's through the cloud deck in about 10 to 14 seconds. No wreck vanishes in the open sky.
- **Through the cloud deck.** Falling through the cloud floor, a wreck tears a hole in it and throws up a ring of cloud; the hole closes over a few seconds. A burning one glows orange under the cloud a moment after she's gone.
- **When several go at once**, the first two get the whole show and any others the flash and the smoke, and at most two ships' masts fall at once, so a phone stays smooth. The smoke and fire from wrecks only take what room is left after the fight's own, so the guns' smoke and sparks never run short.

## The Surge

Hit Surge and the view widens with a jolt and drops back a little (just the same on a phone that's running slowly). Her wings snap wide open with a shiver, pale streaks of wind rush past the ship, her crystals flare and stream blue light up into her sails, a ring of blue sparks bursts from her stern, two white vapour trails pour from her wingtips and hang where she's been, her wake of Aether blazes, and the view rumbles a little until the Surge runs out. Diving close to top speed shows the streaks faintly too. (If your device asks for less motion, the view widens without the jolt.)

## Sound and music

The game is silent until you first touch it (a tap, a click or a key anywhere): phones only let a page make sound after that. From then on:

- **The music is Chris's**, the same pieces as the laptop version: *Thareia* on the title screen, *Market Day* in port, *Sunstone Wind* while you fly between waves (*Gloomfen Drift* over the Gloomfen, *Sunscorch Road* over the Sunscorch Wastes), *Break the Grip* when a wave of raiders arrives, and *The Holder Wakes* for a raider captain, a Man-o'-war, or your ship going down. It only changes at those moments (and between waves when you fly into another region, not more than once in 20 seconds), fading from one piece into the next. It dips for a moment under your broadsides, blasts and banners.
- **Your guns.** Bow guns crack sharply, with a crystal zing on top. A broadside booms down the side gun by gun as it ripples, then rolls on like thunder, deeper for heavier guns (a bigger ship, or more crystal power to the guns). A raider's guns sound a little darker and a captain's louder, and a Man-o'-war's broadside rolls on twice. **Far-off guns sound dull and arrive late, like thunder**: you see the flash, then hear the boom. Sounds on your left come from the left.
- **Hits.** A woody knock and splinters on a hull, a ripping tear in sails, a glassy shatter on crystals, so you can tell what you hit by ear. A hit on your own ship thuds through the deck. A near miss whizzes past on the side it passed.
- **Ships going down.** A deep blast and groaning timbers, then smaller blasts along her hull; crystals dying in falling bells; a treasure ship rings her bell as she strikes. Each wreck falls away with a long rush of air, a whump as she breaks through the clouds and, if she was burning, a muffled boom below them. When your own ship goes down, everything goes dull and far away.
- **Little rewards.** Each shard you gather chimes, higher up the scale for each one of a run; spilled shards clink; the Surge fills the sails with a rush, and as the next one charges a hum climbs to full and a soft ping says it's ready; locking on clicks; a broadside loaded again clacks twice (when you're not holding the trigger).
- **Flying.** The wind rises with your speed and height, with gusts; the rigging whistles at full speed and in a Surge; the crystals hum, higher as you climb (wavering near the Thinning, sour when they're cracked); timbers creak, more in hard turns or when she's badly hurt; fire crackles when she's burning. Inside a cloud the wind goes soft and muffled.
- **The weather.** A storm showing on the horizon rumbles low and far off; as it rolls in, Chris's *Aether storm* (rolling thunder with an eerie ringing shimmer); rain hisses while it pours, and the wind blows harder; each lightning strike is followed by Chris's *thunderclap*, from where it struck, a moment later and duller the further off it was; each gust brings Chris's *wind*.
- **Chris's sound effects** for the big moments: setting sail and coming home, a wave arriving (an alarm, a boss's brass for a captain or a Man-o'-war, a pirate's phrase for a treasure ship), a wave beaten, your ship lost, alarm bells when the hull or the crystals are badly hurt, and a soft chord with a far bell when you cross into another region. In port: buying a ship (coins and a clunk), each upgrade, the skies, and the menu buttons; the crystal power slider plays a note for each notch, high and airy towards Sails, low and metallic towards Guns.
- **Pausing** quiets the sounds and dulls the music. **Leaving the page** (another app, another tab, the phone locked) silences everything until you come back.
- **Settings** (the gear on the title screen and in port, or Settings on the pause card): **Sound on**, and sliders for the **Music** and the **Sounds**. They change at once and are kept on that device only, so a phone can be quiet while a laptop plays. With the sound off nothing is played, and nothing is saved up for later either, so turning it back on gives just a tick, not a burst of old sounds. With the Music slider at nothing the music stops (and starts again when you turn it up); with the Sounds slider at nothing the game makes none of its sounds, so both save a phone's battery. On a phone the card adds: no sound? Check your phone isn't on silent, and its volume is up. (The card's other settings: see [Settings](#settings).)

How it stays smooth on a phone: the game's own sounds are made once, in a moment after the first touch, as 35 short recordings (no sound files; about 2.5 MB of memory), so a big fight plays recordings instead of building hundreds of sounds a second. Each kind of sound has a few voices at once (fewer on a phone: six for your guns and five for the raiders'); a new sound takes the place of the least important one playing (the farthest), and a broadside landing all at once swells one sound rather than ten. A broadside plays four to six booms however many guns it has (so a Man-o'-war's 24 cost what five do). Everything goes through one mix that rides the loud moments and gently rounds off any peak (it never goes past 0.98 of full volume); the check plays the worst fight, with Chris's effects of the same moments and every slider at the top, and makes sure it stays under 0.95 going in, so the rounding never has to squash anything and it never crackles. The sound runs on its own thread, and the game spends well under a thousandth of a second on a volley's sounds and a few hundredths of a thousandth each frame on the music and the sky (the check measures it).

## How each ship flies

How fast a ship goes, how tight it turns and how quickly it climbs all come from its stats in `docs/ships.md`, then its upgrades and crystal power (and, for your own Galleon and Man-o'-war, your crew: see [The Galleon and the Man-o'-war](#the-galleon-and-the-man-o-war)):

- top speed: 14 m/s, plus 3.2 m/s for each point of **speed**
- turning: 0.06, plus 0.028 radians a second for each point of **turning**
- climbing: 3 m/s, plus 2.1 m/s for each point of **climbing**

Measured by `npm run check`, with no upgrades and no wind, 20 seconds at full sail, turning and climbing the whole time:

|---|---|---|---|
| Skiff | 142 km/h | 383° | 460 m |
| Cutter | 166 km/h | 320° | 339 m |
| Brig | 131 km/h | 257° | 299 m |

- A ship eases into a turn or a climb, the way a heavy ship answers its wheel, and leans into its turns.

- The crystals' lift fades near the Thinning (2,400 m), so that's the ceiling. The lowest you can fly is 60 m.

## Damage

Each shot hits whatever it meets first: the sails (one zone per mast), the crystals (each furnace column and its crown of gems), or the hull (the shape it was built from, up to the rail). What damage does, following `docs/ships.md`:

- **Hull**: at zero, the ship goes down. A ship going down rolls over, nose-down, and falls away below the clouds before she's gone (at least 150 m, if she was flying low). A ship below half hull trails smoke from where she was hit, thicker and darker as the hull goes; below a quarter flames burst from her worst wounds (see [Battle scars](#battle-scars)). The crew falls with the hull (for boarding, later).
- **Sails**: torn sails slow the ship, down to 30% of its top speed with none left, and make it turn badly, down to 45%.
- **Crystals**: cracked crystals climb badly, down to 25%, and can't lift as high. Below half, the ship starts to sink, faster as they go. At zero, the ship sinks out of the fight, and a few seconds later drops away below the clouds.

## The raiders

The raiders fly the Captain's classes, by the same rules: the Skiff, Cutter, Brig and Frigate always, and the Galleon and the Man-o'-war only once you own that one (Chris: giant raiders only once you own one, whichever ship you sail that voyage). They're easy to tell apart: **rust-red sails, darker planks and crimson pennants with a black hoist**, and a red wake behind them (a raider captain and a treasure ship have colours of their own: see [Ships that move like they're alive](#ships-that-move-like-theyre-alive)). Each has a tag over it with its class, its distance and its three health bars; off screen, the tag waits at the edge with an arrow pointing to it (all of it on the screen, however long its words: "Treasure · Galleon"). While she readies a broadside her tag flashes red with "Broadside!" in place of her bars, and a ring closing beside it, over her or at the edge. A wave's raiders come in on the horizon, right where its banner shows, so while the banner is up the tags under it fade away, and they come back as it fades (one saying "Broadside!" always shows). A tag never covers a raider's ship far off, its own or another's, and never goes under the panels at the top of the screen (the compass, your ship's panel, the map, the score, the guns' label). Tags that would land on each other, or on a ship, stack upward over the ships, so every ship in a crowd shows under all the tags; where there's no room above (a crowd near the top of a phone held sideways, or a raider close by with her masts high), a tag moves to one side of the stack, or under her ship, and glides there rather than jumping. It stays there while it's clear, and comes back over her once there's room. A flashing one is always drawn on top, so you can read which raider is about to fire. A raider's crystals, lanterns, gun muzzles and weak points glow at the same size as yours on every screen, laptop or phone, upright or sideways.

- **Skiffs and Cutters make attack runs.** They come at you bow-first, firing their bow guns, then after 10 to 15 seconds, or when they get close, peel away side-on and come round again. That's when they're open to your broadside.
- **Brigs, Frigates and the Man-o'-war fight broadside.** They come alongside a few hundred metres off, on whichever side you're on, and fire whole sides. **Her gun ports glow red just before a broadside**, so you can see it coming.
- **The treasure ship runs** (see Treasure ships, above).
- On Crosswinds they're a little weaker than the Captain: they sail at 92% of their class's top speed, reload half as slowly again, aim a little off (up to 2 m for every 100 m to you), and hold their fire until you're within about two-thirds of their reach. Before each broadside their gun ports glow and their red fan shows for 0.8 s, and they aim then, where you're heading (see [Tactics](#tactics)); their broadside crews reload a fifth quicker than their light guns', so they fire their sides as often as before the warning made them wait (about every 3.9 s).
- **On Crosswinds and the Maelstrom they fight smarter**: a Brig, Frigate or Man-o'-war you point your bow at sails for a spot just ahead of it, to cross your bow and rake you with her broadside. On the Maelstrom Skiffs and Cutters also make their attack runs at your stern, where your guns are few, and one your guns are locked on to, with your loaded broadside pointed at her, may suddenly climb or dive out of it. On Fair Winds they fight simply.
- **In the clouds.** While you're inside a cloud, a big one or the cloud floor, raiders aim much worse at you (their shots spread over two and a half times as wide). Deep in cloud you're **hidden**: a raider further off than her skies' sight (180 m on Fair Winds, 250 m on Crosswinds, 350 m on the Maelstrom) can't see you, holds her fire and comes looking where she last saw you, circling there; firing your own guns gives you away for 4 seconds. Raiders hide in cloud too: one deep in cloud more than 250 m off can't be locked on to, and her tag stays where you last saw her, saying "Lost in the cloud", until she readies a broadside (her glowing gun ports give her away).
- They're drawn at the middle or far setting of the detail dial (`docs/ships.md`), whichever suits how big they look on screen.

**How hard it is.** `node tools/sim-fight.mjs skiff 5 cross` sends out a simulated Captain. By herself she's a plain Captain who ignores the tactics: she simply points at the nearest raider and fires round shot, keeps a raider alongside in a ship with broadsides, chases a treasure ship that runs bow-on under full sail (surging when she's getting out of reach of its bow guns), and waits for repairs between waves. She never climbs out of a broadside, never dodges and never hides in cloud (firing all the time would give her away anyway), pays no heed to a raider's warning, never changes shot and never patches her ship. She does fly through the banks of cloud the raiders come out of, where they aim worse at her, and she fights the storm waves in the storm. `--skilled` makes her use the tactics: she climbs on every broadside warning, a third of a second after it starts (as a person would), loads chain shot on a treasure ship within its reach and crystal breakers on a raider captain or a Man-o'-war (from above her), and has her crew patch her when her worst part falls below half. She can sail any of the six ships, owning whichever giants you say (`--owns galleon,manowar`), with upgrades (`--mods 2`), and play several voyages in a row with a summary (`--runs 6`); it says what sank her, whether each treasure ship was caught or got away, how much damage a minute of fighting the raiders' broadsides and light guns did her, and the raking hits each way.

**The tactics** (measured October 9, 24 voyages of each unless it says otherwise):

- **A plain Captain is as hard pressed as before.** In a Brig on Crosswinds, waves 1 to 8, she beats all eight 14 times in 24 (just before the tactics, the same simulated Captain did 15 in 24), the raiders' broadsides doing about 400 damage a minute of fighting (430 before). The raiders' broadside crews reload a fifth quicker so the warning doesn't make their sides any rarer (with them a third quicker, she beat all eight only 8 times in 24), and with Skiffs and Cutters coming at her stern on Crosswinds too she beat all eight only 9 times in 24, so that's left to the Maelstrom.
- **A skilled Captain does far better**: the same Brig beats all eight every time (24 in 24), the raiders' broadsides doing 53 damage a minute, nearly nine tenths less. Climbing on a raider's warning does most of it. A real Captain won't catch every warning with several raiders about, so expect perhaps half that.
- **On the Maelstrom** (warnings of 0.6 s, small raiders at your stern, raiders dodging your broadside), a skilled Captain in a Frigate beats all eight waves 9 times in 12, against a plain one's 3 times in 24, the broadsides doing 40% less than to a plain one (the warning is short there, so she can't always climb out of the way in time).
- **Raking** pays: a plain Captain's broadsides rake raiders now and then as they turn (about 700 raking hits in 24 voyages of a Brig on Crosswinds), and the raiders rake her rarely (about 9, crossing her bow as she comes at them).
- **Chain shot on a treasure Brig, in a Skiff** (wave 6, `node tools/sim-fight.mjs skiff 1 cross 6 --runs 12 --shot auto`: chain shot once she's within its reach): she's caught 9 times in 12, in one and a half to four minutes (2 minutes 10 in the middle of the runs), against round shot's 9 in 12 in under two to three and a half minutes (2 minutes 40). With chain shot she got away twice and the escort sank the Skiff once, as the Skiff kept changing shot between them; with round shot the other three times she was still being chased at five minutes. Chain shot's shorter reach means getting close first.

**Your Galleon and Man-o'-war** (measured October 9, after they were made stronger, on Crosswinds, from wave 10 to wave 15, with no upgrades: `node tools/sim-fight.mjs galleon 6 cross 10 --runs 30`, twice over, and the same for the Man-o'-war; 60 voyages of each). Buying one is a step up at once, and they're still not invincible:

- **The Galleon** (owning the Galleon, so the treasure ships are Galleons and the Man-o'-war's waves have a raider captain's Frigate) beats all six waves nearly two times in three (38 in 60), and nearly always reaches wave 15 (58 in 60). Wave 15, a raider captain's Frigate with a Frigate, a Brig and a Cutter, is where she goes down (20 times in 60; once in wave 10 and once in wave 13).
- **The Man-o'-war** (owning both giants, so waves 12 and 15 bring a raider Man-o'-war) does better again: she beats all six waves more than three times in four (46 in 60). Wave 15, a raider Man-o'-war with a raider captain's Frigate, a Brig and a Cutter, is where she goes down (13 times in 60; once in wave 13).
- **For comparison, a Frigate with every upgrade** (◆ 2,200 and ◆ 7,680 of upgrades) beats all six waves one time in three (8 in 24) before you own the Galleon, and one time in five (5 in 24) once you do (her treasure ships are Galleons then); with no upgrades she sinks in wave 10 every time. Before October 9 a new Galleon beat all six only 2 times in 24, and a new Man-o'-war 6 times in 24.
- **The late waves still win sometimes** (drawn at random; with no upgrades, `node tools/sim-fight.mjs galleon 5 cross 16 --runs 12`, and from wave 21; twelve voyages of each). Your Galleon beats waves 16 to 20 every time (12 in 12, her hull down to a quarter at worst), but gets through waves 21 to 25, five or six raiders at a time, only two times in twelve: she sank nine times (seven of them in wave 24), and once she was still fighting wave 24 when time ran out. Your Man-o'-war meets a raider Man-o'-war in most late waves (32 of 56 waves from 16 to 20) and beats waves 16 to 20 ten times in twelve (she sank once, in wave 18, and was still fighting once when time ran out), and waves 21 to 25 eight times in twelve (she sank in wave 21 twice and 24 once, and was still fighting once). Upgrades make both stronger again.
- Your Galleon runs down wave 6's treasure Galleon and her escort every time, in about a minute and a quarter (8 in 8): laden, the treasure Galleon is the slower ship.

**The Man-o'-war's wave in a Frigate** (October 8, a Frigate with no upgrades): before you own the Man-o'-war, wave 12's raider captain's Frigate and two Cutters are beaten two times in three (9 in 14); once you own her, wave 12's raider Man-o'-war and her two Cutters are beaten four times in five (19 in 24, in one to three and a half minutes; three times she sank the Frigate, and twice it was still fighting her at three and a half minutes), as she's slow to turn and the Frigate keeps clear of her broadside more often than not.

**The treasure Brig** (wave 6, before you own the Galleon; measured late on October 8, `node tools/sim-fight.mjs skiff 1 cross 6 --runs 12`, with the simulated Captain saying whether she was caught): she's caught in a Skiff every time (12 in 12), in one and a half to four and a half minutes; in a Cutter five times in six (10 in 12, in one to four and a half minutes; the other two times she got away, as the Cutter fought her escort first); in a Brig nearly every time (11 in 12), in under a minute to four and a half.

Measured again on October 9, after the tactics, with a plain Captain (24 runs of each; one run is too random to go by; the numbers from late on October 8, before them, in brackets). On Crosswinds it:

- nearly always gets the Skiff through four waves (22 times in 24; 21 in 24), with about ◆ 400 in the hold (enough for the Cutter), and then the wave-5 captain nearly always sinks it (20 of those 22 times; it beat her twice; the other two times it went down in wave 4)
- gets the Brig through all eight waves a little over half the time (14 in 24; 16 in 32) and through seven five times in six (20 in 24; 30 in 32); once the wave-5 captain sank it, three times wave 7's Frigate, and wave 8's Frigate and two Cutters, in a storm, did the rest
- gets the Frigate through seven waves on the Maelstrom seven times in ten (17 in 24; 23 in 30), and through all eight one time in eight (3 in 24; 6 in 30); the wave-5 captain sank it five times, and wave 8 fourteen times. It always beats wave 4's storm, but now that small raiders come at her stern on the Maelstrom, the treasure Brig's escort keeps her busy while the treasure ship gets away more than half the time (10 of 18)

On Fair Winds (`node tools/sim-fight.mjs skiff 5 fair`) it always gets the Skiff through four waves, and gets past the wave-5 captain nearly every time (24 of 24 runs on October 9, with a whole second's warning before each broadside; 28 of 32 on October 8; 22 of 24, and 43 of 48, on October 7). Fair Winds is for learning the ropes, and a brand-new Captain starts there: its raiders hit less than half as hard as on Crosswinds and are about a quarter less sturdy, and a raider captain there is no tougher than her crew, hits no harder and reloads no faster. (It was made easier on October 6 and again on October 7, so the first captain doesn't sink a newcomer.)

A real Captain who uses height, the Surge, the wind, the clouds and the tactics does better than the plain simulated one.

## The code

- `src/game/main.js`: the page; the voyage (and a sea trial), the camera, aiming and locking on, the waves, shards and the HUD (the raiders' tags and the warning kept off your own ship)
- `src/game/port.js`: the port and the void it shows your ship in (with Buy and Try her out), and the title screen's panel (the skies, Set sail, To port)
- `src/game/title.js`: the title screen's sky: your ship flying over the Hearthsea at sunset, the raiders crossing far off, the skies' tint
- `src/game/progress.js`: the save (shards, the six ships, upgrades, skies, best waves, voyages sailed), what each ship costs, and the three skies (a save from before the big two were sold gains them, not owned)
- `src/game/mods.js`: the upgrades and crystal power, and what they do to a ship, and how much better your crew sail the big two (their helm)
- `src/game/world.js`: the map, sky, cloud floor, cloud puffs and region names, and the look of the sky (the voyages' afternoon, the title's sunset and its stormy Maelstrom tint, a storm, each region's air), kept in one set of numbers every part of the sky shares; the towering clouds round the horizon, the glory and her shadow on the cloud floor, the sea's glitter, and the cloud floor's pattern worked out in JavaScript too (where a ship can hide)
- `src/game/sky.js`: the sky at sea and its weather, in one place: each region's air, storms rolling in and clearing, lightning and gusts, the mist inside a cloud, hiding in cloud, the glory and the sun's glare
- `src/game/weather.js`: the weather's pieces, each drawn in one go: rain (or dust, snow or motes), lightning, the mist's veil, scraps of scud and the sun's glare
- `src/game/flight.js`: how a ship flies, for the Captain and the raiders; damage, the wind, the Surge, and the crew patching her
- `src/game/tactics.js`: the fight's own rules: the three kinds of shot (how hard each hits each part, how it flies, its price and its picture), raking fire, patching, and how smart the raiders fight
- `src/game/raiders.js`: the raiders: their colours, their tactics (readying a broadside with its red fan and aiming where you're heading; crossing your bow, coming at your stern, dodging your broadside), captains, bounties and the waves (the giants only once you own them, with their stand-ins until then), and the title screen's raiders far off, each drawn in one piece
- `src/game/pickups.js`: the Crystal Shards a downed raider spills
- `src/game/damage.js`: what a shot hits, worked out from each ship's own model
- `src/game/events.js`: the game's news (a gun firing, a hit, a raider down, a wave starting, a ship bought...), told in one place for the effects and the sound and music to answer; the list of every event is at its top. Anything may stop listening (or start) while the news is being told, without another listener missing it
- `src/game/fx.js`: the effects: sparks, flashes and glows, the gunsmoke and debris from hits, the view's kick and shake, buzzing a phone, and a dial to slow the game's clock for a moment (slow motion)
- `src/game/effects.js`: the smoke (gunsmoke, and smoke and fire from damaged ships: billowing puffs lit by the sun, from one small picture made as the game starts) and the debris (splinters, torn canvas and crystal shards)
- `src/game/wrecks.js`: a raider going down: the chain of blasts, her crystals and sails, falling masts, the smoke column, the hole in the cloud deck
- `src/game/looks.js`: every ship's battle scars and life kept up to date: hits put where they landed, fraying, soot, dimming crystals, listing, flames, patching between waves, and clean again in port; her wings with her sails, her pennants with her speed, her lids and guns with the fight and her volleys, a Man-o'-war's columns marked and blowing out
- `src/ship/dress.js`: how the scars are drawn on a ship's own planks, sails and crystals (shared with the ships demo, with its New, Battered and Wrecked looks), and how her wings, lids, guns and pennants move as she's drawn; `src/ship/flames.js`: the flames from the worst wounds, one draw for the whole sky
- `src/ship/livery.js`: the colours a ship sails in (yours, a raider's, a raider captain's, a treasure ship's), the same in the game and the ships demo
- `src/game/wakes.js`: the wakes of Aether behind every ship, and your vapour trails, one draw for the whole sky
- `src/game/surge.js`: how a Surge looks: the wind streaks, the crystals flaring, the vapour trails
- `src/game/sound.js`: what the game sounds like: the answers to the game's news (guns, hits, wrecks, shards, the port...), which of Chris's pieces plays when, and the sky's own sound
- `src/game/settings.js`: the Settings card (each setting a row; kept on the device), what each picture setting draws, and the aim speeds
- `src/audio/audio.js`: starting the sound with the first touch, stopping it when the page is left, and Chris's music and effects played through the game's own mix
- `src/audio/mixer.js`: the mix: its buses and master, the voices each kind of sound gets, and placing a sound in the sky
- `src/audio/voices.js`: the sounds made for this game (cannon, hits, blasts, chimes...) on the same instruments as Chris's, and making them into recordings
- `src/audio/thareia/`: Chris's music and instruments, exactly as he gave them (shared with the laptop version)
- `src/game/guns.js`: where each gun sits, which battery faces where, aiming ahead of a moving ship, gun weights, the rippling volleys, the shot they're loaded with, the bolts (chain shot's two spinning heads), and the shape of a raider's red fan
- `src/game/input.js`: the keyboard, mouse and touch controls
- `demos/game.html`: the title screen, port and HUD (with the bounties that rise from wrecks), the Settings card, How to fly and the keys; the fonts; the dip through the night between screens; and where everything goes on a laptop, a phone held upright and a phone held sideways
- `tools/map-art.mjs`: packs the nine map tiles into `assets/map/` (run it if the tiles change)
- `tools/sim-fight.mjs`: the simulated Captain, for tuning the raiders: any of the six ships, which giants she owns (`--owns`), her upgrades (`--mods`) and crystal power (`--power`), and several voyages in a row with a summary (`--runs`); a plain Captain, or one who uses the tactics (`--dodge`, `--shot auto`, `--patch`, all three with `--skilled`, and `--policy cross` to cross raiders' bows); it says what sank her, whether each treasure ship was caught or got away, how much damage a minute the raiders' broadsides and light guns did, and the raking hits each way

`npm run check` (or `node tools/check.mjs`) plays the whole game in a hidden browser, the size of a laptop and of a phone, and must end with "all good". In short, it makes sure that:

- **your progress is safe**: it's saved, it follows you between your phone and your laptop, and no voyage's shards are ever lost; a brand-new Captain starts on Fair Winds;
- **every ship is right**: built near its size budget, flying as fast as it should, and every gun firing and hitting;
- **the game plays from start to end**: the title screen, the port (buying a ship and an upgrade), whole voyages, the card between waves, and the keyboard, mouse and touch controls;
- **a sea trial changes nothing**: "Try her out" under a ship you don't own, on a laptop and a phone held either way: the Galleon's trial won (no shards, no giant raiders, only the way home), the Man-o'-war's lost, the Gale's left from the pause card, each home to her in port with what she costs, and the save exactly as it was every time;
- **the Galleon and the Man-o'-war are yours late in the game**: bought with the port's buttons (on a laptop and with taps on a phone) and sailed, all of her in view with the raiders clear above her masts, both decks of her broadside rippling, her helm quicker than a raider's; no giant among the raiders before you own it (every wave to the fortieth, many times over), a treasure Brig at the treasure waves and a raider captain's Frigate at the Man-o'-war's, said so on the card and the banner, each giant after you own her (each on her own too), the Man-o'-war growing common late on and never more than one treasure ship in a late wave; the Man-o'-war sold only after the Galleon; the Buy button on one line at every phone size, all of how she sails above it, her ship clear above the panel, and your own ship's five bars above Set sail; and an old save gaining the big two, not owned;
- **a fight looks and feels right**: broadsides ripple with their smoke, hits are marked, warnings flash, wrecks blow apart, smoke and fall through the clouds, and shards are gathered;
- **the tactics work**: each shot hits each part exactly as hard as its table says; chain shot loaded with 2 reloads every battery, aims at her sails and tears them; five volleys of crystal breakers crack a Man-o'-war's crystals from above and then round shot comes back; chain shot bought in port for ◆ 200, and both earned at sea (a treasure ship striking, a raider captain's Frigate brought down); a raider readies her broadside (red fan, "Broadside!" with its ring, over her and at the edge) the skies' 0.8 s before she fires, and a Captain who climbs on her warning is hardly hit where one who holds still is hit hard; a broadside down a ship's length does half as much again, both ways ("Raked!", and the toast); X patches her worst part by a third in 6 s with the guns reloading at half speed, and not again for 30 s; on a phone the shot button and Patch work with a tap and fit the screen at every size; smarter raiders cross your bow on Crosswinds and dodge your broadside on the Maelstrom;
- **ships show their scars**: a shot leaves its hole where it went through the sail (in that sail only) or struck the planks, a long fight's hits all keep their marks, crystals dim and crack, torn sails fray, badly holed ships list and burn and smoke from their open wounds, your ship is patched between waves (the patches never growing back), spotless in port and clean the next voyage, three raiders of one class cost no more drawing work than one, and a raider burning 200 m off shows her flames and her holes clearly, on a laptop and a phone (and the ships demo's Battered and Wrecked show it, its buttons fitting a phone held sideways with the whole ship in sight above them as she turns);
- **storms and clouds work**: where the game reckons the cloud floor is thick enough to hide in matches the cloud as drawn, the right waves storm (rain, lightning, gusts, thicker cloud) and clear again, a storm on its way is a billowing wall of cloud (lumpy all along its top, lit on top, dark at its foot, moving, with no seam due north), a storm's rain comes in gently even over dust, every third wave's bank of cloud shows where the game thinks it is and goes once the wave is beaten, the mist shows inside a cloud and goes out of it, raiders aim worse at you in a cloud, hiding works and your broadside gives you away, a raider lost in cloud can't be locked on to (her tag giving how far off you last saw her) until she readies a broadside, each region's air comes in, and the weather costs a phone little (pictures of each into `shots/`);
- **ships move like they're alive**: the wings fold with the sails and snap open in a Surge, the pennants follow her speed, the lids open as a wave comes (and burst open with the first gun when you fire between waves), each gun of a broadside kicks back exactly as it fires and runs out once loaded, a raider opens her lids before her broadside, a shot through a folded wing holes it where it went through, every wake is drawn in one go and stays soft (never burnt out to white), a raider's wake far off is a long, clear, steady streak, a raider lost in cloud shows nothing over it (no wake), the captain's and treasure ship's colours cost no new drawing work, a treasure ship's glints move with her, a wave with a treasure Brig is announced as a treasure ship's and her hold is worth a Galleon's ◆ 300, a Galleon captain comes in close and across your path, and a Man-o'-war's column blows out once. In the ships demo, as the graphics card draws them, the lids swing out from the hull and each side's guns kick back on their own, and on a phone it never holds more than two sets of ships;
- **the sound is right**: it plays when it should and never too loud, and Chris's music and sound files are exactly as he gave them;
- **the screen is tidy**: nothing covers anything else on laptops and phones of several sizes (upright and sideways, down to 568 by 320, the first voyage's banner on two lines at most), in a battle, between waves, on the big map, in port and on the title screen; in each of the six ships, seen from behind, her side and her bow, no raider's tag and not the warning lies on your own ship, and the warning still shows clear of the crosshair, the panels and the tags; raiders' tags far off, two or three close together, cover none of their ships and stay off the panels at the top, and tags at the edge never land on each other (in a crowd all round, as the view swings round, and with three raiders behind you on a phone held sideways), as does the tag of a raider close by with her masts high; the widest tags (a treasure Galleon's, a raider captain's) all on the screen at its edge; a wave's banner never drawn over its raiders' tags; the ships demo's fleet card on a phone with no word broken in two; and a raider's glows are the same size as yours on every screen;
- **it stays smooth on a phone**: the effects keep to their budgets, and the title screen is drawn in fewer goes than a voyage. Frames are compared timed in turns (A, B, then B, A, six times), going by the middle turn, so a machine busy with other work can't trip the comparison.

It saves pictures of the title, the port and a battle into `shots/`, and of the weather (`shots/sky-*.png`: a storm on the horizon, rain and lightning, inside a cloud and coming out, hiding in the cloud floor, the towering clouds, the glory, the Sunscorch Wastes' air, a raider coming out of a bank of cloud). The whole list of what it checks, in detail, is at the top of `tools/check.mjs`.

`node tools/check.mjs --quick` plays only the game page at laptop size, for checking during work; it ends with "all good (quick)". `node tools/check.mjs --ships` checks only the ships demo (a couple of minutes). The full check is the one that counts; it says at the end how long it took (about 15 minutes on this machine's software drawing, more while it's busy with other work, down from about 25: the ships demo is now drawn only for the pictures and pixels the check looks at, instead of a second or more a frame the whole time, and the sky at sea isn't drawn while the real keys, mouse and touches are tried).
